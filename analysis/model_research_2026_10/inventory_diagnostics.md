# Partial pickups and residual inventory (Experiment J): findings

The full tables are in `results_J.md` and `results_J.csv`, both generated by `exp_inventory.py`. The analysis uses champion residuals on 18,324 scored pickups, in core mode, over 2018–19 and 2021–26. Research only.

## 1. Is there a carryover signature? No.

The test removes the model's own feedback by predicting pickups k−1 and k from **one fixed rate** (the one known before k−1). Under that design:
- If short pickups left oil behind to be collected next time, the log errors of consecutive pickups would be **negatively** correlated.
- Shared error in the rate alone would make them positive.

| Window | Pairs | r (fixed rate) | 95% interval (customer clusters) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| dev 2021–23 | 6,201 | +0.41 | +0.36 to +0.45 |
| confirm 2024–26 | 8,675 | +0.29 | +0.25 to +0.34 |
| 2018–19 | 3,126 | +0.31 | +0.25 to +0.36 |

The correlation is strongly **positive** in every window and every gap bucket. It is weakest at 1–14-day gaps (+0.20), the only place a carryover component could be hiding.

The same holds when conditioning on the previous pickup's size:
- **After a large pickup** (> 1.4 × the customer's median): the next pickup is also large, and the champion **under**-projects it by 9%.
- **After a small pickup** (< 0.6 ×): the champion over-projects by 5%.
- Partial pickups would produce the reverse pattern.

**Observed:** consecutive pickups move together. Production shifts persist, and the rate catches up slowly.

**Not identifiable:** a small carryover effect could exist underneath that persistence. Pickup records alone cannot separate the two. Per the plan, no carryover or allowance correction was tested, because the precondition (a stable negative signature) failed.

## 2. Short gaps are under-projected and long gaps over-projected

| Days on the clock | Pickups | WAPE | Bias (95% interval) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1–14 | 2,891 | 30.9% | −15.3% (−19.9 to −12.0) |
| 15–21 | 3,560 | 27.5% | −7.9% |
| 22–30 | 3,944 | 28.8% | −6.7% |
| 31–45 | 3,937 | 32.7% | −3.3% |
| 46–60 | 1,899 | 36.7% | +5.1% |
| 61–90 | 1,426 | 48.1% | +17.7% |
| 91–180 | 667 | 80.4% | +60.9% (+46.8 to +79.1) |

Regressing log(actual ÷ predicted) on log(days ÷ the customer's usual gap) gives the elasticity of collected gallons to days:

| Window | Implied β (1 = gallons ∝ days) | 95% interval |
|---|---:|---|
| dev 2021–23 | 0.48 | 0.43–0.53 |
| confirm 2024–26 | 0.53 | 0.47–0.58 |
| 2018–19 | 0.46 | 0.40–0.51 |

**Collected volume grows with about the square root of the gap, not in proportion to it.** The effect is the same in every window, including the two least-inspected years.

## 3. What that means: collected volume vs production

The model assumes collected gallons = rate × days. The data say a pickup that comes early finds more than that, and one that comes late finds less. Possible explanations:

1. **Pickup timing is not independent of fill level.**
   - The truck comes early when someone calls or sees a full barrel.
   - It stays away from stops it knows are slow.
   - So a long gap is partly *evidence* of low production, and a short gap of high production.
2. **Containers cap what can be collected.** After a long gap the barrel was full and the overflow, or the oil the restaurant disposed of some other way, is never recorded.
3. **Production changed during the gap**, the persistence seen in §1.

None of the three is carryover. Only (2) describes oil that existed and was not collected. (1) and (3) are about rates, not inventory.

A collected-volume model was scored to size the effect. It is not an inventory model:
- Prediction = rate × usual gap × (days ÷ usual gap)^β, with β chosen on dev from {1.0, 0.8, 0.6, 0.4}, which picked **0.6**.
- All years: −2.51 points (−3.16 to −1.92), 8 of 8 years better. Confirm −1.51, 2018–19 −4.75.
- 91–180-day pickups: −27.5 points, bias +61% → +22%. 1–14-day pickups: bias −15% → −5%.
- **Bias overall goes from −2.0% to −5.0%.** Total under-projection grows, and under-projection is the costlier error for this business (README, 2026-10-08).

**Do not read this as "a barrel unvisited for 120 days holds √ of what the rate says."** The β is measured on pickups that happened, and when they happened was chosen by people who could see or hear about the barrel. A stop that has been silent for a long time *and has not been visited* is exactly the case the data cannot speak to. Applying β to today's projections would push long-unvisited stops down the urgency list. If explanation (2) is right, those are the barrels most likely to be overflowing.

## 4. Other cuts

- **Stage:** new customers over-project by 9.3% (WAPE 45%). Established −3.7%, seasonal −4.7%, closers −1.5%.
- **Shared barrels:** −2.8%, no different from single accounts (−1.9%).
- **Previous pickup vs today's capacity (hindsight; no capacity history exists):** no pattern. After a pickup at ≥ 90% of capacity the next bias is −2.6%, within noise of the rest.
- **Accounts with no listed capacity:** WAPE 46% and bias +6%. They are mostly newer or irregular accounts.

## Verdicts

| Question | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Partial pickups / carryover explain short-gap under-projection | **Not supported.** No negative lag signature in any window; carryover not identifiable beneath the persistence |
| Fixed allowance / carryover correction | **Not tested** (precondition failed, as planned) |
| Collected volume ∝ days | **Rejected as a description of collections**: β ≈ 0.5 in every window |
| Use β in projections | **Not recommended.** It predicts collected volume given the truck's timing, not oil in the barrel. A candidate for **range bands** (asymmetric by days on the clock) and for a question to Jim about how stops are timed |
