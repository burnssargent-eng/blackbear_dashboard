# Closer open-day exposure (Experiment G): findings

The full tables are in `results_G.md` and `results_G.csv`, both generated by `exp_closer.py`. Research only.

## What production already does

`rate_open_season` (BPT:707) pools the last ≤ 6 gaps that lie **wholly** in open months. Every day it divides by is an open day. **G1 ("exclude gaps crossing closed seasons") is the champion**, so it was not re-implemented.

Three facts frame the rest:
- **The first pickup after reopening is never projected.** The row is held out as "season open — awaiting first pickup" until a pickup inside the season. G therefore cannot score it; Experiments H and I will.
- **Season comes from the detector** (core mode), at month level.
- **Coverage fallback is empty here.** Every in-season ranked closer row in 2018–26 had ≥ 3 wholly-open gaps.

## What it says about closers

- **Cross-season gaps carry less oil than their open days imply. This is the clearest finding here.**
  - G2 counts the gap that spans a closure at gallons ÷ days in open months, and comes out biased −26%.
  - The first pickup of a season is the one that ends such a gap. It holds about three quarters of what "open days × in-season rate" predicts, consistently across summer and winter closers and across years.
  - That is the opposite of a carryover story: no leftover oil is evident at reopening.
  - **Observed:** the shortfall.
  - **Plausible explanations:**
    - the month-level calendar counts days the business was not really open (it opens mid-month, or winds down before its last month);
    - a slow start to each season;
    - the last pickup before closing drained the barrel.
  - The records cannot separate these explanations.
- **So closer errors look more like wrong operating dates than wrong rates.** The in-season rate is fine: G0 is near unbiased (−1.5%), and swapping in this season's gaps only (G4/G5) changes nothing useful.
- **This season's gaps only (G4) is noisier, not better.** Two or three in-season gaps are too few. The pooled open-season history is the more stable estimate, which supports the existing "≥ 3 gaps" rule.

## False urgency (weekly snapshots, partially observed)

Every Monday from 2018–19 to 2026, every detected closer:
- "Flagged" means the projection is at least the customer's median pickup over its last 6 pickups. There is no capacity history, so capacity cannot be used.
- A flagged week with no pickup in the next 30 days is *possible* false urgency. The truck may simply not have come, so this is not ground truth.

| Set | Model | Customer-weeks ranked | Flagged | Flagged with no pickup in 30 d |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Off-season, ranked | G0 (champion) | 444 | 214 | **188 (88%)** |
| In season | G0 | 1,817 | 458 | 172 (38%) |
| In season | G2 / G3 / G4 / G5 | 1,817 | 257 / 340 / 492 / 481 | 37% / 27% / 37% / 37% |

- **An operational finding outside G's scope: the off-season "called recently" rule.**
  - Detected and semi-closers are ranked off-season when their last pickup is within max(60, 2 × median gap) days.
  - They are then projected on the stage rate, which runs on through the closed months.
  - 214 such customer-weeks were flagged urgent. **88% had no pickup within 30 days**, against 38% in season.
  - Only 49 of the 444 ranked off-season weeks had any pickup within 30 days.
  - This is the strongest false-urgency signal in the study. It comes from routing, not from the rate formula, and is a question for H (reopening and holdout rules).
- G3 flags less and has the lowest no-pickup share in season (27%), but its accuracy is worse, so the lower share is not evidence it is better.

## Limitations

- 27 customers and 350 pickups in total, with only 25 pickups in 2018–19. Intervals are wide.
- The season is month-level by construction, so true open and close dates are not observable (see H).
