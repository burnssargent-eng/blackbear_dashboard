# Seasonal exposure (Experiment C): findings

The full tables are in `results_C.md` and `results_C.csv`, both generated by `exp_seasonal.py`. Research only; the builder is unchanged.

## What production already does

Half B of the seasonal formula already performs C1/C5:
- It divides the last-3 rate by the **day-weighted** index over the days those pickups covered.
- It multiplies by the day-weighted index over the days being projected (`cf.gap_weighted`), including intervals that cross month boundaries.

So the open questions were narrower:
1. Is the **index** right? It is built by spreading each pickup flat over at most 60 days.
2. Should **half A** (last year ±3 weeks, last 2) also be put on an exposure basis?
3. Should half B use more gaps?

## Interpretation

- **The 60-day spread cap is a real but small distortion.**
  - Spreading a long gap's gallons over all of its days, not just the last 60, gives a slightly better index in every replay year on the wide scope.
  - On the strict production scope it is neutral on confirm (−0.07). It is noticeably better only in 2022, where dev's −5.8 comes from.
  - Operationally that is about 0.4–0.8 WAPE points on seasonal customers, which is a few gallons a stop. It is more faithful, not more useful.
- **Fitting the index to the intervals by EM (C-idx) changes the shapes a lot but not the accuracy.**
  - The fitted shapes differ visibly from production's. The Blue Stone's June peak flattens, for example.
  - Accuracy moves inside noise: confirm −0.09, all years −0.84 with an interval spanning zero.
  - It is better on high-amplitude customers (−3.0) and worse on low-amplitude ones (+1.6), so it trades accuracy between groups rather than adding any.
  - It also needed a numerical floor. The unfloored fit drives poorly observed months to zero and blew up on 2018–19 rows. The floor (0.10, the scope's own trough rule) was added **after seeing those rows**. That is disclosed, and it is one more reason C-idx's 2018–19 gain should not be counted.
- **Exposure-adjusting half A hurts everywhere (0 of 8 years).**
  - Rescaling last year's ±3-week window and the last-2 rate by an index ratio adds index noise to two terms that are already season-aligned. Last year's window sits at the same date, and the last 2 pickups are recent.
  - "More physically faithful" here means more parameters to be wrong, not better predictions.
- **Six gaps in half B (C2) helps the long intervals and hurts the short ones.**
  - 3+-month intervals: −4.1. One-month intervals: +1.4.
  - On strict it is better in all 6 scored years, but the pooled interval just touches zero (−2.60 to +0.01).
  - It is a candidate for prospective monitoring, not adoption.
- **Does exposure improve predictions or only their interpretation?** Production already captures most of the gain through half B. What is left (index construction) moves accuracy by under a point. The seasonal formula's remaining error is not a timing-of-production problem.

## Limitations

- The strict sample is small: 55 dev pickups, 21 from 2018–19. Seasonal-stage numbers for 2018 cannot exist, because the test needs 3 years from 2016 on.
- Classification is unchanged in every candidate; only the rate ingredients change.
- C-A, C2 and the C3/C4 references are compared on the same rows as the champion. A candidate with no rate falls back to the champion's, and those counts are in the CSV `note` column.
