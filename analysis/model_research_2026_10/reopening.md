# Off-season and reopening (Experiments H and I): findings

The full tables are in `results_H.md`, `results_I.md` and the matching CSVs, all generated by `exp_reopen.py`. Research only. The season is the detector's (core mode, month level). The snapshot outcomes are **partially observed**: "no pickup within 30 days" does not prove the barrel was not full, and when the truck came was partly the routing's own doing.

## H. Off-season "called recently" (highest ROI in this study)

When a detected closer is off-season but had a pickup within max(60, 2 × median gap) days, production ranks it and projects **stage rate × every day since the clock**, closed days included.

| Weekly snapshots, all eval years | Flagged (projection ≥ median pickup) | Flagged, no pickup in 30 d | Pickup weeks caught |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| In season, champion (reference) | 408 | 34% | 269 of 1,113 |
| Off-season ranked, champion | 214 | **88%** | 26 of 49 |
| Off-season ranked, **H2**: closer rate × open days only | 59 | 75% | 15 of 49 |
| All off-season, **H1**: hold out, as a true closer is | 0 | – | 0 of 82 |

- By window, the champion's off-season false-flag share is stable: 87% dev, 87% confirm, 100% 2018–19.
- **H2 removes about three quarters of the off-season flags.** In confirm they fall from 158 to 37, and the no-pickup share from 87% to 62%. The cost: 7 fewer of the 32 confirm pickup-weeks get flagged.
- Off-season pickups are call-driven: the customer rings and the truck comes. A projection-based flag adds little to a call, which is why the lost catches cost less than the counts suggest.
- **On the off-season pickups that did happen** (17 scored, 13 customers, too few for windows), the champion over-projects them by +171%. H2 cuts that to +64%: Δ −97 points (−183 to −51), 12 of 13 customers better.
- **H1** (hold everything off-season) is simplest, but it unlists 82 weeks that preceded a pickup.
- **Verdict:** H2 is supported directionally in every window and is the smallest change. It reuses the closer rate and the season months the row already has, and counts only open days. It is a routing and urgency change for about 25 customers, not an accuracy change on the main list.

## H. Ranking during the "awaiting first pickup" wait

Production holds a closer out from the season's 1st until its first in-season pickup, a median of **70 days** (quartiles 52–88).

Ranking those weeks (R1, projected closer rate × days since opening + L):
- flags 376–601 weeks;
- **63–66% of the flags have no pickup within 30 days** (the in-season reference is 34%).

**Verdict:** keep the holdout. The month-level opening does not carry enough timing information to rank on.

## I. The first pickup after reopening

There are 67 first-of-season pickups across 27 customers in the eval years. Production never projects them. The reference is I1(0): closer rate × days since the 1st of the opening month.

- **I1(0) over-projects badly:** WAPE 147%, bias +94%.
- **Counting oil left over from last season (I0) makes it worse in 7 of 8 years:** +59 points (+26 to +107).
- **Regression:** actual = a × (rate × this-season days) + b × (rate × last season's tail days).

| Window | a (this season) | b (last season's tail) |
|---|---|---|
| All eval years | 0.30 (0.19–0.37) | 0.13 (−0.01 to 0.90) |
| Dev | 0.35 | — |
| Confirm | 0.20 | — |

- **Carryover is not identifiable.** b's interval spans 0.
- **The season yields about a third of what its calendar days imply before the first pickup.** That is consistent with G's finding that cross-season gaps fall short, and stronger here, because these gaps are the ones that contain the opening.
- **A late-start lag helps a lot but does not fix it:**
  - Lag 30 d (dev-chosen, at the **edge** of the declared grid {0, 14, 30}): −58 points (−85 to −32), 8 of 8 years better.
  - Bias stays at +21%.
  - Rolling picks: 30 from 2019 on.
  - The edge pick and a ≈ 0.3 together imply an effective opening about 6–7 weeks after the month-level one. **That was not tested and would be fitted, not chosen.**
- A fixed allowance (+0.25 or +0.5 × median pickup) does not beat the lag alone.

**Verdict:** the first pickup of a season is not forecastable from month-level seasons. The plausible causes cannot be separated from pickup records:
- the business opens later than the month boundary;
- it ramps up slowly;
- Jim's first visit is scheduled rather than driven by the barrel.

This supports the holdout and is a question for Jim: **when do these accounts actually open, and how is the first visit of a season timed?** An operating-dates field in the registry would answer it directly. No production change is recommended from I.
