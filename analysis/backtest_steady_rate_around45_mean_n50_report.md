# Steady-rate backtest — ±45 days around the pickup — every other pickup in that window, plain mean of their rates. USES FUTURE PICKUPS: a benchmark, not a forecast

Generated by `analysis/backtest_steady_rate.py --around 45 --rate mean --sample 50`.
Percentages are in percent units.

## Method

For each test pickup *x*:

- every **other** pickup dated within 45 days either side of *x* contributes its own rate: gallons ÷ days since its previous pickup
- **rate** = plain mean of those rates
- *x*'s own interval is excluded — its rate is gallons(*x*) ÷ gap, which would hand the model the answer
- **prediction** = rate × (date(*x*) − date(*x-1*))

Test pickups are from 2023-01-01 onward. Every test pickup has at least
8 prior pickups, so all models are scored on the same pickups.
Up to 50 tests per customer are drawn at random (seed 42, a fresh
generator per customer). "Full" columns rerun the model over every eligible test.

Input is `oil_collections_raw.csv`, which holds only qualifying pickups:
`EMPTY_QTYS` {0,1,2,3} are removed upstream and 4-gallon records count as 4.

## Caveats

- **Not a forecast.** This window reaches forward in time, so the rate uses pickups that have not happened when *x* is being predicted. Read it as the error that remains even with the true local production rate — the noise floor any real model is measured against.
- **MAPE punishes small pickups.** WAPE and median APE are the steadier reads
  when a customer has occasional small pickups.
- **Pickup size is not production.** A pickup reflects how full the container
  was when the driver arrived, which depends on the route schedule as much as
  on how much oil the customer produced.
- **Hand-picked sample.** These customers were chosen because they look steady.
  Results are a best case, not an estimate for the customer base as a whole.
